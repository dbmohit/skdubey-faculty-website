About & Research Philosophy

Sarvesh Kumar Dubey is an Associate Professor at the Centre for Atmospheric Sciences, Indian Institute of Technology Delhi (from June 2026). He joined CAS as an Assistant Professor in June 2019. His research primarily focuses on advancing atmospheric modeling through numerical and data-driven frameworks, with particular emphasis on understanding multi-scale interactions in the tropical atmosphere.

His work integrates theoretical modeling, numerical experimentation, and observational analysis to explore the energy budget and nonlinear scale interactions across phenomena such as intraseasonal oscillations (MJO/BSISO), the Indian summer monsoon, ENSO, synoptic systems, and organized convection.

During his doctoral and postdoctoral research, he contributed to the development of advanced global modeling frameworks aimed at improving the representation of atmospheric dynamics in Earth System Models. At IIT Delhi, his research group continues to bridge large-scale modeling and data-driven diagnostics to address fundamental questions in tropical meteorology and climate variability.

Research Themes & Contributions

Geodesic Grids & Dynamical Cores

Formulation and evaluation of advanced conservative multi-tracer transport schemes (such as FF-CSLAM) and hydrostatic/non-hydrostatic dynamical cores (DYNAMICO, MPAS) on icosahedral-hexagonal meshes, avoiding pole singularities and conserving global properties.

Nonlinear Scale Energetics

Pioneering spectral kinetic and available potential energy exchange diagnostics between the Madden-Julian Oscillation (MJO), equatorial waves (MRG), synoptic disturbances, and monsoon low-pressure systems across spatial and temporal hierarchies.

Tropical Cyclones & Rapid Intensification

Energetics of vortex-scale and sub-vortex-scale asymmetries in cyclogenesis. Investigating environmental drivers, moisture fluxes, and rapid intensity changes of cyclones over the Bay of Bengal and Arabian Sea.

Machine Learning & Data Assimilation

Developing ML-driven super ensembles for operational weather prediction (NCMRWF), hybrid background error covariance matrix formulations, and deep learning for lightning-assisted thunderstorm intensity prediction.
